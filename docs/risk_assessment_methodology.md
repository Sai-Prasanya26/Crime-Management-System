## 1. Purpose & Core Principles

The Crime Risk Assessment engine provides a deterministic, transparent, and explainable assessment of jurisdiction-level operational risk. In contrast to opaque black-box machine learning models, the risk scoring system is designed as an **algorithmic composite index** grounded in empirical crime intelligence and verified forecasts.

### Core Architectural Principles:
1. **Explainability**: Every score must be mathematically decomposable into its constituent factor contributions, enabling command staff to identify *why* a jurisdiction is categorized as high or critical risk.
2. **Determinism & Reproducibility**: Given the same underlying historical incidents, census demographics, and production forecasts, the algorithm produces the exact same score.
3. **Safety & Demographic Neutrality**: The system strictly excludes sensitive personal characteristics (victim age, gender, religion, race) and avoids using past police deployment counts to prevent algorithmic bias and self-fulfilling enforcement loops.
4. **Operational Actionability**: Balances absolute resource demand (large urban volume) against per-capita intensity (crime density per 100k citizens) and localized temporal momentum (recent acceleration).

## 2. Assessment Granularity & Unit of Analysis

- **Primary Unit of Analysis**: **District** ($640$ Census historical administrative districts across all $36$ States and Union Territories).
- **Temporal Frequency**: **Monthly Assessment Period** (e.g. `2026-01-01`).
- **Temporal Duality**:
  - **Observed Baseline (Historical)**: Trailing 12-month historical incident volume ($2025\text{-}01$ to $2025\text{-}12$).
  - **Anticipated Pressure (Forward Forecast)**: 1-month-ahead operational prediction from the production HGBR model (`crime_predictions`).

## 5. Mathematical Formulations of Selected Risk Factors

For a given district $d$ at assessment period $t$ (e.g. `2026-01-01`):

### 1. Forecasted Crime Volume ($F_d$)
The production HGBR forecast for the target period:
$$F_d = \hat{y}_{d, t} \quad \text{from } \texttt{crime\_predictions}$$

### 2. Historical Crime Volume Baseline ($V_d$)
Sum of actual reported crime incidents over the preceding 12 calendar months:
$$V_d = \sum_{m=1}^{12} y_{d, t-m}$$

### 3. Population-Normalized Crime Rate ($R_d$)
Annual incidents per 100,000 citizens using the Census 2011 population baseline:
$$R_d = \left( \frac{V_d}{\max(\text{Population}_d, 1)} \right) \times 100,000$$

### 4. Recent Crime Trend Ratio ($T_d$)
Ratio comparing the immediate 3-month quarterly volume ($V_{\text{recent}} = \sum_{m=1}^3 y_{d, t-m}$) against the preceding 3-month quarter ($V_{\text{prior}} = \sum_{m=4}^6 y_{d, t-m}$):
$$T_d = \frac{V_{\text{recent}}}{V_{\text{prior}} + 1.0}$$
*Smoothing parameter $\epsilon = 1.0$ prevents division by zero in zero-incident periods.*

## 6. Normalization Methodology: Percentile Rank Normalization

Empirical analysis revealed that the raw crime rate exhibits an extreme **skewness of 7.49** and **kurtosis of 79.77** due to remote mountain districts with tiny populations ($\le 20,000$). Standard linear Min-Max normalization compresses 95% of districts below score 5.0.

To solve this, **Percentile Rank Normalization** is applied independently across all $N = 640$ districts for each factor:

$$\text{Pct}(X_d) = \left( \frac{\text{Rank}(X_d) - 1}{N - 1} \right) \times 100$$

### Mathematical Properties:
1. **Dynamic Range Guarantee**: Maps every factor strictly to $[0.0, 100.0]$.
2. **Variance Equality**: Every factor attains an identical mean ($\mu \approx 50.0$) and standard deviation ($\sigma \approx 28.9$).
3. **Outlier Immunity**: Extreme positive spikes in small districts are capped at the 100th percentile without distorting the remaining 639 districts.

## 7. Weight Selection Rationale & Multi-Collinearity Mitigation

The four normalized factors are partitioned into three conceptual operational dimensions:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 CRIME RISK COMPOSITE INDEX                  │
├──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. SCALE & VOLUME (50%)      │ - Forecast Pressure (30%)    │
│                              │ - Historical Baseline (20%)  │
├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. PER-CAPITA INTENSITY (30%)│ - Crime Rate / 100k (30%)    │
├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. MOMENTUM & VELOCITY (20%) │ - 3-Month Trend Ratio (20%)  │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Scale Dimension ($50\%$)**: Combines near-term AI forecast ($30\%$) and verified historical baseline ($20\%$). Because Pearson correlation between $F$ and $V$ is $0.9857$, limiting their combined weight to $50\%$ prevents sheer population scale from overwhelming per-capita intensity.
- **Intensity Dimension ($30\%$)**: Guarantees that smaller districts experiencing severe localized per-capita crime density receive proportional priority.
- **Velocity Dimension ($20\%$)**: Completely orthogonal ($r \approx 0.00$), ensuring emerging crime surges are captured before annual totals accumulate.

## 8. Final Composite Risk Score Formulation

The overall composite crime risk score for district $d$ is:

$$\text{Risk Score}_d = 0.30 \cdot \text{Pct}(F_d) + 0.20 \cdot \text{Pct}(V_d) + 0.30 \cdot \text{Pct}(R_d) + 0.20 \cdot \text{Pct}(T_d)$$

Rounded to 2 decimal places: $\text{Risk Score}_d \in [0.00, 100.00]$.

## 9. Transparent Risk Bands

The composite score maps to four operational risk tiers:

### A. Primary Operational Thresholds (Standard Quadrants):

| Score Range | Risk Level | Operational Meaning | Recommended Administrative Action |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **$75.00 \text{--} 100.00$** | **CRITICAL** | Severe multi-dimensional pressure across volume, rate, and accelerating trend | Strategic headquarters intervention, emergency patrol redeployment, multi-team task forces |
| **$50.00 \text{--} 74.99$** | **HIGH** | Elevated operational demand or intense per-capita density | Enhanced surveillance, specialized investigation allocation, target hardening |
| **$25.00 \text{--} 49.99$** | **MODERATE** | Typical baseline crime activity consistent with national median | Standard police precinct operations, routine patrol schedules |
| **$0.00 \text{--} 24.99$** | **LOW** | Subdued crime volume, low per-capita rates, decelerating trends | Baseline preventive policing, opportunity for mutual aid resource lending |

### B. Empirical Distribution Characteristics:
Under this formula across the $640$ districts:
- **Mean Score**: $50.08$ | **Median**: $50.41$ | **Std**: $13.67$
- **Min Score**: $5.13$ | **Max Score**: $80.54$
- **Low ($<25$)**: $23 \text{ districts } (3.6\%)$
- **Moderate ($25\text{--}49$)**: $290 \text{ districts } (45.3\%)$
- **High ($50\text{--}74$)**: $314 \text{ districts } (49.1\%)$
- **Critical ($\ge 75$)**: $13 \text{ districts } (2.0\%)$

*(Note: Because the sum of 4 independent uniform variables follows an Irwin-Hall distribution centered at 50 with $\sigma \approx 14$, scores cluster naturally between $25$ and $75$. The top $2\%$ strictly isolates districts experiencing simultaneous severe pressure across multiple dimensions).*

## 10. Explainability & Factor Attribution Framework

Every calculated risk score is accompanied by an automated **Factor Contribution Profile**:

1. **Normalized Component Percentiles**:
   $$\{ F_{\text{pct}}, V_{\text{pct}}, R_{\text{pct}}, T_{\text{pct}} \}$$
2. **Weighted Contribution Breakdown**:
   $$\{ 0.30 \cdot F_{\text{pct}}, 0.20 \cdot V_{\text{pct}}, 0.30 \cdot R_{\text{pct}}, 0.20 \cdot T_{\text{pct}} \}$$
3. **Dominant Risk Driver Attribution**:
   The primary driver is identified as the factor with the highest percentile rank:
   $$\text{Primary Driver} = \arg\max_{i \in \{F, V, R, T\}} \text{Pct}(X_{i, d})$$

### District Archetype Profiles:
- **Metropolitan Volume Hub** (e.g. Thane, Mumbai Suburban):  
  *High Forecast ($100\%$) + High Volume ($100\%$) + Low Rate ($9\%$) $\rightarrow$ Score: $66.58$ (High Risk)*  
  *Driver: Elevated Forecasted Operational Volume*
- **Remote Small-Population Outlier** (e.g. Dibang Valley):  
  *Low Forecast ($2\%$) + Low Volume ($0.2\%$) + High Rate ($100\%$) $\rightarrow$ Score: $31.45$ (Moderate Risk)*  
  *Driver: High Per-Capita Victimization Rate (tempered by low absolute volume)*
- **Emerging Escalation Hotspot** (e.g. Chatra):  
  *Moderate Volume ($23\%$) + Moderate Rate ($28\%$) + High Trend ($100\%$) $\rightarrow$ Score: $40.63$ (Moderate Risk)*  
  *Driver: Rapid Recent 3-Month Crime Surge*
- **Multi-Dimensional Critical District** (e.g. Alwar):  
  *High Forecast ($96\%$) + High Volume ($94\%$) + Moderate Rate ($50\%$) + High Trend ($90\%$) $\rightarrow$ Score: $80.56$ (Critical Risk)*  
  *Driver: Multi-dimensional Convergence across Volume, Forecast, and Trend*

## 11. Database Schema Alignment (`crime_risk_scores`)

The existing MySQL table `crime_risk_scores` is fully compatible with this methodology without requiring schema redesign:

```sql
CREATE TABLE `crime_risk_scores` (
  `id` bigint NOT NULL AUTO_INCREMENT,
  `district_id` int NOT NULL,
  `period_year` smallint NOT NULL,
  `period_month` smallint NOT NULL,
  `overall_risk_score` decimal(5,2) NOT NULL,   -- Composite Score [0-100]
  `risk_level` enum('LOW','MODERATE','HIGH','CRITICAL') NOT NULL,
  `severity_index` decimal(5,2) NOT NULL,       -- Auxiliary category severity ratio
  `trend_index` decimal(5,2) NOT NULL,          -- Normalized Trend Percentile (T_pct)
  `volume_index` decimal(5,2) NOT NULL,         -- Normalized Volume/Forecast Percentile
  `population_density_factor` decimal(5,2) NOT NULL, -- Normalized Rate Percentile (R_pct)
  `calculation_version` varchar(20) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'v1.0',
  `generated_at` timestamp NOT NULL DEFAULT (now()),
  PRIMARY KEY (`id`),
  UNIQUE KEY `uq_district_risk_period` (`district_id`,`period_year`,`period_month`)
);
```

## 13. Limitations & Future Extensions

1. **Decennial Census Demographics**: Per-capita crime rates rely on Census 2011 population figures. Future iterations can integrate dynamic Aadhaar-linked or satellite-derived population projections.
2. **Absence of Type-Level Forecasting**: Forecasting is currently aggregated at the monthly district level; severity weighting can be elevated in the composite score once multi-task crime-type forecasting is developed.
3. **Inter-District Border Spillovers**: Current risk scoring treats districts independently; spatial econometric models (e.g. spatial lag / spatial error models) may be evaluated in future enhancements.
